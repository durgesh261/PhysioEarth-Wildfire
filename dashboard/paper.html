PhysioEarth: A Physics-Constrained Fourier Neural Operator for Coupled Reaction-Diffusion Wildfire Spread with Topographic Conservation

Climate AI Research Initiative • Master's Candidate Portfolio • UN SDG #13 Climate Action
ABSTRACT Wildfires have intensified globally due to climate extremes, destroying over 10 million hectares annually and demanding rapid, accurate operational forecasting. While numerical fluid-dynamics simulations (e.g., WRF-SFIRE) provide high physical fidelity, their computational latency prevents real-time evacuation planning. Conversely, conventional deep learning models (such as 2D U-Nets or ConvLSTMs) produce severe physical violations—including fuel regeneration, negative temperatures, and barrier breaching. We introduce PhysioEarth-Wildfire, a scientific machine learning framework integrating a 2D Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) with physics-informed conservation constraints. By formulating wildfire spread in the spectral frequency domain and penalizing unphysical fuel generation, PhysioEarth achieves a 39% reduction in temperature field RMSE and reduces river firebreak breaches from 16.55% down to 0.98% compared to unconstrained baselines.

1. Introduction & The Global Crisis

Climate change has exacerbated the frequency and intensity of megafires across the Mediterranean, North America, Australia, and South Asia. Operational emergency response relies heavily on numerical fire simulators based on the Rothermel surface spread model. However, these solvers compute step-by-step partial differential equations on high-resolution meshes, requiring hours on computational clusters.

In recent years, deep learning has been explored as a surrogate modeling tool. However, pure data-driven vision models suffer from critical failure modes: they lack inductive physical priors. In multi-step rollouts, error accumulation leads to "phantom fires" igniting across fuel-free firebreaks (e.g., rivers and rocky canyons) and models spontaneously regenerating burnt vegetation fuel.

2. Mathematical Formulation & Governing Equations

We model wildfire propagation as a coupled Reaction-Diffusion and Combustion system:

∂T / ∂t = ∇ · (D ∇T) - veff · ∇T + Scombustion(T, Y) - γ T
∂Y / ∂t = - kburn Y · 1{T > Tign}

where T is the thermal temperature field, Y ∈ [0, 1] is remaining biomass fuel fraction, and veff is the Rothermel-derived effective advection vector combining atmospheric wind and topographic slope acceleration:

veff = cwind w + cslope ∇h ||∇h||
