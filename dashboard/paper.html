TopoCollapse: Quantifying Manifold Degeneracy and Tail-Loss in Recursive Generative AI via Persistent Homology

Antigravity Research Lab • Master's Candidate Portfolio • durgesh261/TopoCollapse
ABSTRACT Recent advances in generative modeling have unveiled the phenomenon of Model Autophagy (Model Collapse): the recursive training of generative models on self-generated synthetic distributions induces severe degradation, variance shrinkage, and loss of diversity. While classical distribution metrics such as the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) measure statistical moments, they implicitly rely on Gaussian assumptions, leaving them largely insensitive to the non-convex topological erosion of complex manifolds. In this work, we propose TopoCollapse, a geometric framework utilizing Vietoris-Rips Persistent Homology to evaluate structural degeneration across recursive generative cycles. By analyzing the lifespans of 1-dimensional homological loops (H1), Persistent Entropy, and diagram Bottleneck distances (W_inf), we demonstrate that topological invariants detect manifold boundary collapse with a +177% divergence signal across 6 recursive generations, during which conventional Fréchet distance metrics remain virtually stationary (FD ≈ 0.002 - 0.003).

1. Introduction & Research Motivation

The ubiquitous deployment of large-scale generative models has populated digital media with synthetic artifacts. As subsequent generations of models scrape and ingest web-scale corpora, they unavoidably ingest the outputs of prior model generations. Shumailov et al. (Nature, 2024) demonstrated theoretically and empirically that this recursive self-consumption triggers Model Collapse, leading to irreversible degradation where rare events and minority data modes are systematically purged.

Conventional generative evaluation metrics rely heavily on distribution divergences such as the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), which presumes latent features follow a multivariate normal distribution. When a generative model experiences early-stage collapse, the centroid vector μ and global covariance trace Tr(Σ) undergo minimal displacement, concealing the localized extinction of delicate geometric boundaries, internal cavities, and disconnected clusters.

4. Key Findings & Discussion

The Blindness of Moment Metrics: Across all 6 generations, the Fréchet distance fluctuates harmlessly between 0.0018 and 0.0036. Because the ring's symmetry centers at the origin, the first-order mean does not shift, and the global covariance trace is largely unaffected by boundary erosion.

Topological Monotonicity: In contrast, the Bottleneck distance surges from 0.0741 to 0.2055 (+177%), clearly tracking the destruction of the annular cavity and the systematic erosion of minority mode boundaries.
